Oscar Best Picture Nominees vs. Ratings from 2010-2026


In 2009, the Oscars telecast was coming off a historic ratings slide. Viewers had gone from a record high of 55 million in 1998 (the year "Titanic" won best picture) to a low of 32 million in 2008, and some publicly speculated that Oscar-nominated films had become too niche and inaccessible to the general public. Hoping to capitalize on the popularity of high-grossing movies like "The Dark Knight" and "Wall-E" (both of which were excluded from the 2009 best picture nominations), the Academy of Motion Picture Arts and Sciences president Sid Ganis announced that, starting in 2010, the number of best picture nominees would be expanded from five to ten.

The question is: did the plan work?

Were the nominees for best picture more popular in the aftermath of the decision? And did Oscar telecast ratings improve as a result of the change?
The following interactive plots can help us explore these questions.



The plot below displays the inflation-adjusted domestic box office of the best picture nominees from 2010 to 2026. Nominees from individual years are grouped together by color, and the height of the dots indicates the total domestic box office of each film — higher dots mean higher box office totals.
